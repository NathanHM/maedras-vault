*Collected by Scholar [[Thyren Valis]] of the Ivory Spire*  

Throughout the lands of [[Rulif|Rulif]], [[Qavrud|Qavrud]], [[Alithi|Alithi]], and [[Tayan|Tayan]], the natural world is steeped in legend. Vines and flowers, with their uncanny ability to thrive in both life and death, have long been symbols of power, sacrifice, and renewal. The following myths may offer insight to travellers who walk the paths between wild groves and forgotten shrines.  
## 1. The Thorned Crown  
**Origin**: [[Alithi|Alithi]] Noble Folklore  
**Summary**:  
The tale speaks of a long-forgotten monarch who ruled [[Alithi|Alithi]] during a time of terrible drought. Desperate to save her people, the queen sought the guidance of a hidden druidic circle. They warned her that the land’s curse could only be broken if she wore a crown of living thorns.  
The queen bound a crown of black vines to her head and bled freely. The rains returned, but when her court sought to remove the crown, the vines had rooted into her skull. The queen’s body was buried beneath the [[Everthorn Grove]], where twisted vines still grow unnaturally thick.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
Some scholars believe the “living thorns” were a rare magical plant known as [[Bloodthorn]], rumoured to feed on sacrifice and flourish only in places of grief. The [[Everthorn Grove]] remains an overgrown ruin, and local rangers report unnatural whispers from its depths.  
## 2. The Vine that Hung the World  
**Origin**: [[Qavrud|Qavrudian]] Sailor’s Tale  
**Summary**:  
Sailors tell of a vast vine that coils through the earth’s core — a living chain that holds the world in place. According to legend, this vine is woven from the souls of forgotten kings and queens who swore to bind the world against the rising dark.  
The myth warns that if the vine is severed, the world will be cast adrift, and the dead will rise from the earth’s heart to reclaim what was lost.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
The imagery of a binding vine appears repeatedly in [[Qavrud|Qavrudian]] murals. Some theorists connect this legend to the River’s Chain myth from [[Tayan|Tayan]], suggesting both are metaphors for [[ley lines]] or ancient magical conduits.  
## 3. The Bloom of First Light  
**Origin**: [[Worship of Vel'Enar (Rulif)|Church of Vel'Enar]] Doctrine  
**Summary**:  
A revered legend within the [[Worship of Vel'Enar (Rulif)|Church of Vel'Enar]] tells of a divine flower that bloomed at the dawn of creation. The Ember Bloom, said to bear petals of pure flame, emerged from the ashes of the world’s first fire. Legends claim the flower’s light guided [[Vel'Enar]] to shape the cycle of life and death.  
Clerics say that when the bloom's petals fall, they transform into fiery feathers that carry prayers directly to [[Vel'Enar]]'s court. Some sects claim that the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]] seeks to twist the Ember Bloom's power to awaken their [[The Emberheart|god]].  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
The Ember Bloom may symbolize a potent magical artifact or lost ritual. Given the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Cult]]’s focus on flame and rebirth, relics tied to this legend may be crucial in understanding their goals.  
## 4. The Garden of Endless Tears  
**Origin**: [[Tayan|Tayan]] Funeral Tradition  
**Summary**:  
It is whispered that deep in the [[Tayan|Tayan]] mountains lies a hidden garden where grief itself blooms. The legend tells of a powerful guardian spirit, the Weeping Warden, who tends a field of silver flowers known as Mourning Lilies. Each lily is said to represent the unspoken grief of a forgotten soul.  
The legend warns that should these flowers ever be plucked, the spirit’s tears will flood the mountain passes, drowning those who dared disturb the dead.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
The legend of the Weeping Warden is often dismissed as superstition, yet travellers in the highlands report spotting pale flowers growing in forgotten cairns and shrines. Some whisper that the lilies can be brewed into a potent elixir capable of stirring memories of the departed.  
## 5. The Vine of Whispering Hands  
**Origin**: Folk Legend from [[Stormhaven]]  
**Summary**:  
Dockside tales in [[Stormhaven]] tell of a parasitic vine known as the [[Gravegrasp]]. According to legend, this vine coils through forgotten tombs, feeding on the memories of the dead. When the wind stirs its leaves, the whispers of lost souls are said to call out to travellers, promising forgotten secrets.  
The myth warns that those who listen too closely risk becoming vessels for restless spirits, their minds overtaken by the memories the vine devours.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
While likely exaggerated, some herbalists claim the [[Gravegrasp]] is real — a rare plant whose leaves can be brewed into a potent mind-enhancing elixir. However, prolonged use is said to cause unsettling visions and fragmented memories.  
## Conclusion:  
These myths, though often allegorical, may prove valuable to those navigating the lands of [[Rulif|Rulif]], [[Qavrud|Qavrud]], [[Alithi|Alithi]], and [[Tayan|Tayan]]. The persistence of vine and flower symbolism throughout multiple cultures suggests a deeper connection to forgotten magics — and perhaps even to the mysteries of the [[Transport Circles]] or the rise of the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]].
