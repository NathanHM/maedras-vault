*Compiled by Scholar [[Thyren Valis]] of the Ivory Spire*

The myths of our world, passed through song, scripture, and whispered warnings, hold curious truths — distorted by time yet worthy of study. Below are five notable tales that may offer guidance to those who explore our realms.  
## 1. The Pyre's Breath  
**Origin**: Vath'iran Oral Tradition  
**Summary**:  
It is said that long ago, a flame was born beneath the earth — a fire that could not die. Legends claim it was once a fallen star, buried deep by ancient hands. Those who touched its light found visions of forgotten places and distant lands.  
The myth warns that the flame's power may still burn within forgotten halls — a beacon for those foolish enough to seek it. [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|The Eternal Ember Cult]] believes this star to be the heart of their god, [[the Emberheart]], imprisoned yet immortal.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
In my research, I have uncovered [[Vath'ira|Vath'iran]] etchings describing a "starfire bound in stone" that corresponds to this myth. The presence of scorched ruins across [[Vath'ira|Vath'iran]] territories suggests that those seeking to control this power often faced ruin themselves.  
## 2. The Silent Watcher  
**Origin**: [[Qavrud|Qavrudian]] Merchant Lore  
**Summary**:  
Merchants crossing the northern mountain passes speak of a towering stone figure known as the Silent Watcher. According to legend, the statue was once a warrior who defied the gods. As punishment, the gods cursed him to stand eternal, watching the world he once sought to rule.  
Travelers claim the statue's gaze shifts with the seasons, always staring toward something unseen — perhaps a forgotten treasure or an ancient curse yet to awaken.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
The Silent Watcher myth appears remarkably consistent across multiple cultures. I suspect the statue may have connections to pre-[[Vath'ira|Vath'iran]] civilizations. Some travellers claim strange runes line its base — possibly tied to the transport circles.  
## 3. The Mother's Tithe  
**Origin**: [[Motherhood of Vel'Enar|Church of Vel'Enar]] Canon  
**Summary**:  
An old parable within the [[Motherhood of Vel'Enar|Church]] tells of a grieving mother who pleaded with [[Vel'Enar]] to spare her sick child. In desperation, she burned her family's harvest in offering. The child recovered, but the mother was cursed to hear the whispers of her lost crops in the wind — forever mourning her sacrifice.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
Though moral in tone, this tale bears striking similarities to reports of the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]]'s beliefs. Their practice of fire-based sacrifice may have drawn from this tale's themes. Additionally, references to “whispers in the wind” mirror descriptions of the Whispering Ashes found in the [[Ashenhold]].  
## 4. The River's Chain  
**Origin**: [[Tayan|Tayan]] Mountain Lore  
**Summary**:  
A tale often told among [[Tayan|Tayan]] herders speaks of a hidden river said to bind the earth itself. Legends claim the river's current is invisible, yet those who wade through its waters feel an unseen pull — as if something is dragging them onward. The myth warns that if the river's course is broken, "the earth's bones will shatter."  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
Curiously, [[Tayan|Tayan]] fortress ruins show carvings of serpentine waterways engraved into stone — symbols resembling elemental glyphs tied to transport circles. Some theorists believe this “river” may represent ley lines or magical conduits.  
## 5. The Cradle of the Forgotten  
**Origin**: [[Stormhaven]] Dockside Tales  
**Summary**:  
Sailors speak of an island that surfaces only during violent storms — a jagged spire of rock known as the Cradle of the Forgotten. According to legend, the island was once a prison for a monstrous entity that could "steal the warmth from men's hearts."  
The myth claims that those who set foot on the island hear voices calling their names. Few return from such voyages, and those who do describe halls lined with blackened iron and flickering flames that whisper forgotten truths.  
**Scholarly Notes**:  
The mention of “blackened iron” and flame motifs strongly suggests a link to the [[Sunken Sanctum]] and the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]]. If true, the Cradle of the Forgotten may be one of the cult's forgotten outposts or perhaps a failed attempt to contain [[the Emberheart]] itself.  
## **Conclusion**:  
While myths are often dismissed as mere superstition, these stories bear curious connections to confirmed histories and forgotten ruins. The wise adventurer will remember that folklore often conceals forgotten truths — and that the greatest dangers are often the oldest.
