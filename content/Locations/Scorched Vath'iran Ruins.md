The Scorched Ruins the players explored were once the thriving city of [[Vath'ira]], a prosperous settlement that stood as a hub of learning, trade, and magical innovation. Nestled between key trade routes, [[Vath'ira]] was renowned for its artisans, scholars, and mystical archives — a place where rival nations once sent envoys to negotiate trade and settle disputes.
## The Fall of [[Vath'ira]]
The city's destruction is deeply tied to the rise of the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]]. Long before the cult declared itself openly, its agents infiltrated [[Vath'ira]], spreading whispers of [[the Emberheart]] — a god of flame, rebirth, and divine judgment. The cult preached that [[the Emberheart]] would purge corruption from the world, and they sought to unlock its power.

Rumours claim that beneath the city lay an ancient vault — one of the first transport circles ever constructed. The [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|cult]] believed this vault contained relics linked to [[the Emberheart]]’s imprisonment, including a potential key to unlocking the god's prison in the [[Sunken Sanctum]].

In their zeal to awaken their fiery deity, the cult triggered a catastrophic magical backlash. Some accounts claim the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]] ignited a ritual that spiralled out of control; others whisper that [[the Emberheart]]’s power was briefly unleashed. Regardless, the result was the same — a searing inferno engulfed the city, leaving behind twisted stone, melted iron, and endless ash. Survivors described the flames as unnatural, moving like serpents that sought out life to consume.

The destruction of [[Vath'ira]] was not a sudden blaze but a slow, orchestrated descent into ruin — a perfect storm of politics, religion, and misguided ambition that played into the [[Cult of the Eternal Ember|Eternal Ember Cult]]’s plans.
## Present-Day Ruins
The ruins now bear the scars of that fire — charred foundations, warped stone streets, and the skeletal remains of its inhabitants scorched into walls like grim murals.
